    Motor and Control Model¶

    This chapter gives the equations of the plant and of the prototype controller, in the order the code computes them. The plant is implemented twice, in Scilab (model/) and in portable C (sil/c/sstep_plant.c), and the controller prototype follows Stepper-C. Tests compare them.

    Rule: a change of the model is made in sstep_plant.c and in the Scilab prototype together, and a change in Stepper-C is ported to the prototype and to the Python twin in the same step.

    Motor¶

    The state is \(x = [i_A, i_B, \omega, \theta]\) in A, A, rad/s and rad, with \(p = N_s / 4\) electrical turns per mechanical turn.

    \[ \begin{aligned} L \frac{di_A}{dt} &= v_A - R\, i_A - e_A, & e_A &= -K_t\, \omega \sin(p\theta) \\ L \frac{di_B}{dt} &= v_B - R\, i_B - e_B, & e_B &= K_t\, \omega \cos(p\theta) \\ J \frac{d\omega}{dt} &= T_e - T_L - T_f \\ T_e &= K_t \left(-i_A \sin(p\theta) + i_B \cos(p\theta)\right) - T_d \sin(4 p\theta) \\ T_f &= B\, \omega + T_c \tanh(\omega / \omega_c) \\ \frac{d\theta}{dt} &= \omega \end{aligned} \]

    \(T_d\) is the detent torque, with one period per full step, and \(T_c\) is the dry friction, smoothed below \(\omega_c\), so the model has no stiction. Both are 0 by default, because the datasheet does not give them. The power balance \(e_A i_A + e_B i_B = T_e\, \omega\) holds without detent torque (test T00).

    Convention. With \(i_A = I \cos\varphi\) and \(i_B = I \sin\varphi\) the rotor settles at \(p\theta = \varphi\). Increasing \(\varphi\) turns the rotor forward, and phase A leads phase B by 90°. Stepper-C uses the same convention.

    Current loop¶

    Every fast period \(T_s = 1 / f_s\), ctrl_period() does the following, as the fast loop of Stepper-C:

    1. Sample \(i_A\), \(i_B\).

    2. Compute the references and the back-EMF feed-forward at the commanded speed \(\omega^*\):

      \[ i_A^* = I \cos\varphi, \quad i_B^* = I \sin\varphi, \quad v_{ff} = K_t\, \omega^* \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\varphi \\ \cos\varphi \end{bmatrix} \]

    3. Run one PI controller per phase, with pole-zero cancellation, so the closed loop is of first order with bandwidth \(\omega_c = 2\pi \cdot\) bw_hz:

      \[ v = K_p\, e + \textit{integ} + v_{ff}, \quad K_p = L\, \omega_c, \quad K_i = R\, \omega_c \]

    4. Limit the voltage to what the power stage can give, scaling the vector and keeping its direction (bridge_avg()). With two H-bridges, \(\lvert v_A \rvert, \lvert v_B \rvert \le V_{bus}\). With the shared leg, \(\max(a, b, 0) - \min(a, b, 0) \le 1\) for the duty differences \(a\) and \(b\), which gives a circular amplitude of \(V_{bus} / \sqrt{2}\).

    5. Update the integrator with back-calculation, \(T_t = L / R\):

      \[ \textit{integ} \leftarrow \textit{integ} + K_i T_s\, e + \frac{T_s}{T_t} (v_{sat} - v) \]

    6. The voltage is applied in the next period, the one-period computation delay of the MCU.

    7. The prototype integrates the motor over \(T_s\) with RK4, with ctl.nsub sub-steps.

    Motion generator¶

    motion_init() and ref_motion() reproduce the integer algorithm of the slow loop of Stepper-C. Positions are in 1/65536 microstep, and velocities in 1/65536 microstep per slow period. The commands are [t kind value]: 1 MOVE target, 2 RUN velocity, 3 STOP, and in the prototype also 4 LOAD torque.

    Every slow period:

    • The velocity changes by at most acc when speeding up, or dec when slowing down.

    • A move aims at the largest \(v_{new}\) for which this period's distance \((v + v_{new})/2\) plus the braking distance \(v_{new}^2 / (2\, dec)\) fits into the distance left:

      \[ v_{new} = \frac{-dec + \sqrt{dec^2 + 8\, dec\, \lvert rem \rvert - 4\, dec\, \lvert v \rvert}}{2} \]

      This is exact for the discrete profile: no overshoot, and an exact stop on the target. The last period stops from a velocity at or below 2 dec. - The fast loop advances the electrical angle by a constant increment per period, and it is set again every slow period. One electrical turn is \(2^{32}\), and one microstep is \(2^{30} / M\). - The run current applies while moving, and the hold current after hold_delay without motion.

    Test X03 compares this generator with the Python twin.

    Power stage¶

    brd.stage selects the model, in the prototype and in the plant block.

    Averaged¶

    "avg" is the default: each leg produces its mean voltage, duty × \(V_{bus}\). It is fast and is used by most tests.

    With brd.leg_error = stage_active().leg_error every leg also gets the voltage error measured with ngspice against the leg current, linear between the table points. This covers dead time, body diodes and leg resistance, at the speed of the averaged model. The table is valid at its operating point (stg.vbus, stg.fs). The default [] is ideal legs.

    Switching¶

    "switching" splits every PWM period at the switching instants of the centre-aligned legs, from counter peak to counter peak, which is between two current samples.

    • Every turn-on is delayed by the dead time dead_ns.
    • During the dead time the body diode carries the current, so the leg is at \(-V_d\) or \(V_{bus} + V_d\), depending on the current direction.
    • Conducting switches drop \(R_{ds(on)}\, i\).

    It shows the current ripple, the peak current per period and the voltage error of the dead time:

    \[ \Delta v = -2\, \mathrm{sign}(i)\, (V_{bus} + 2 V_d)\, \frac{t_d}{T_s} \]

    per phase. The current loop removes this in steady state, but it distorts the current near zero crossings. The ripple on a locked phase is

    \[ \Delta i_{pp} = (V_{bus} - R I)\, \frac{\lvert v_A \rvert}{V_{bus}}\, \frac{T_s}{2 L} \]

    The results are in Measured Results.

    Stall detection¶

    stall_init(), stall_observe(), stall_check() and stall_status() are the detector of Stepper-C. ctl.stall_time_ms > 0 switches it on, and after the fault the outputs are off and the reference is frozen, as in the library.

    Fast loop. The back-EMF comes from the limited voltage. The voltage computed two calls ago is the one applied from sample \(k-1\) to \(k\):

    \[ e_k = v_{k-2} - \left(\frac{L}{T_s} + \frac{R}{2}\right) i_k + \left(\frac{L}{T_s} - \frac{R}{2}\right) i_{k-1} \]

    It is saturated at \(\pm V_{bus}\), rotated into the commanded frame (\(d\) along the current, \(q\) 90° ahead), and filtered by a first-order low-pass with the time constant stall_filter_us.

    Slow loop. In integer millivolts, against the command of the period that just ended, with \(E = \lvert K_e\, \omega \rvert\), sync = stall_sync_pct, and \(e_s\) the back-EMF at stall_speed:

    Case The condition is true when
    In motion, \(\lvert v \rvert \ge\) stall_speed \(\mathrm{sgn}(v)\, e_q < \frac{sync}{100} E\), with \(e_d\) between \(-(E/3 + R I / 10)\) and \(E/3 + 2 R I / 5\): a rotor that does not turn
    Reversed back-EMF \(\mathrm{sgn}(v)\, e_q < -(E/2 + e_s)\) keeps the condition true for \(N/2\) periods
    At rest or slow The deviation of \((e_d, e_q - K_e \omega)\) from its running mean, with a time constant of \(N\) periods, is larger than \(e_s\)

    An up/down counter rises while the condition is true and falls otherwise. The fault trips at \(N\) = stall_time_ms in slow periods. With ctl.protect = 1, an overcurrent trip while the counter is above zero reports both SSTEP_FAULT_OVERCURRENT and SSTEP_FAULT_STALL.

    The results and the limits of the detector are in Measured Results.

    The Python twin has no stall detector; the X tests run with detection off.
